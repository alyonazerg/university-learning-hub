# Examination questions EVOLVE 6 (Units 7–9)

## UNIT 7 — Roots

1. How much can learning about ancestry tell us about who we are? What might a family tree or DNA test reveal? What might it fail to explain?

2. Would you want to take a DNA ancestry test? What benefits could it offer? What privacy or interpretation concerns would you consider?

3. Which has a greater influence on identity: genes or upbringing? How might the two interact? Support your view with a general or invented example.

4. Can discovering unexpected information about ancestry change someone’s sense of identity? Why might different people react differently?

5. What makes a family or community tradition meaningful? How can people keep it alive without following every detail exactly?

6. When can family celebrations bring people together, and when can they create pressure? How can different expectations be handled respectfully?

7. What gives a place special significance? How can returning to a place affect someone’s feelings about their roots? You may use an invented example.

8. Should endangered languages be actively preserved? What could be lost if a language disappears? What practical difficulties should be considered?

9. How can a cultural celebration be promoted to people who know little about it? Which methods would be effective? How could organisers avoid oversimplifying its meaning?

10. Do family ties create stronger obligations than friendships? Are there limits to those obligations? Explain and qualify your view.

11. What can families pass down besides genes and possessions? Which patterns are useful to keep, and which might people choose to change?

12. Our roots influence us, but they do not define us. To what extent do you agree? Discuss inherited background, personal choice, and the possibility of change.

## UNIT 8 — Short

1. What most often distracts you when you need to concentrate? Why is it difficult to ignore? Which strategies help you regain focus?

2. Does your ability to concentrate depend more on your interest in a task or on the environment? Compare two situations and explain.

3. People often claim that modern technology has shortened our attention spans. How convincing do you find this claim? What evidence would you want to see?

4. How do you prioritise a long to-do list? What can happen when you switch repeatedly between tasks? How could your approach be improved?

5. When is it sensible to trust a gut reaction? When would careful analysis be more useful? Give examples of both types of decision.

6. Have you ever changed your first impression after learning more? Why can instincts be useful but also misleading? You may use an invented example.

7. How would you make an important decision with limited time and incomplete information? Which factors would you consider first? Explain.

8. What makes an app genuinely useful rather than simply engaging? Which features would persuade you to use it? What would make you stop using it?

9. Could an app that controls other apps help people concentrate? What advantages and drawbacks might it have? Would you try one?

10. What makes a product description persuasive? How can someone highlight selling points without exaggerating? Give an example.

11. What should a short presentation to potential investors include? How would you make the main idea clear and memorable? Which details would you leave for questions?

12. If you could pitch one app or service for students, what problem would it solve? How would it work? Why would people choose it over existing alternatives?

## UNIT 9 — Healthy vs. Modern Life

1. How can a modern routine make it difficult to stay physically active? Which habits contribute to a sedentary lifestyle? What realistic changes could help?

2. Is sitting for long periods mainly an individual responsibility or a problem that workplaces and universities should address? Explain your view.

3. What could a university do to encourage movement during the day? Which measures would be practical? What barriers might there be?

4. Why do people sometimes sacrifice sleep even when they know it matters? How can study, work, and screen time affect sleep habits?

5. What advice would you give a student who struggles to get enough sleep? Which changes could be realistic during a busy week? How would you avoid oversimplifying the problem?

6. Should organisations change schedules to support healthier routines? What benefits and disadvantages might later starts or more flexible hours create?

7. What environmental factors can affect health in a town or city? Which should local authorities prioritise? Explain your reasoning.

8. When a public official avoids a difficult question, how can a journalist ask for a clearer answer? What makes a probing question fair rather than aggressive?

9. How can traffic or industrial activity affect a community’s quality of life? What competing interests need to be considered? Suggest a balanced response.

10. Why is access to clean water more than a health issue? How can it affect education, work, and opportunities? Explain with a general example.

11. What might make a clean-water initiative sustainable? How should charities, businesses, and local communities share responsibility? Discuss possible difficulties.

12. How would you choose a healthier routine or service for a group of people with different needs? What trade-offs would you explain? How could you judge whether the choice was successful?
