# Situations EVOLVE 5 (Units 7–9)

## UNIT 7 — Priorities

### Conversation Situation 1: Meaningful Work or a High Salary

**Situation:**
You are advising a graduate who has two job offers: one pays well but is not very interesting; the other is meaningful but pays less. Agree on which offer to recommend.

**Student A:**
You think financial security should come first. Explain the benefits of the higher salary and respond to your partner’s concerns.

**Student B:**
You think fulfilment and personal values should come first. Explain the benefits of meaningful work and suggest a realistic compromise.

### Conversation Situation 2: A Purchase Worth Making

**Situation:**
You have a limited budget and are deciding how to spend it. You can buy a useful object or pay for a memorable experience. Agree on one choice.

**Student A:**
You prefer a practical object that can be used for a long time. Explain its value and question the benefits of the experience.

**Student B:**
You prefer an experience that can create lasting memories. Explain its value and respond to concerns about practicality.

### Conversation Situation 3: Negotiating a Better Deal

**Situation:**
A customer wants to buy a second-hand bicycle, but the asking price is above their budget. Negotiate a deal that both people can accept.

**Student A:**
You are the customer. Ask for a lower price, give reasons, and offer a compromise.

**Student B:**
You are the seller. Explain why the bicycle is worth the price, respond to the offer, and decide what you can include in the deal.

### Conversation Situation 4: An Impulsive Purchase

**Situation:**
One of you wants to buy an expensive item immediately because a website says the offer will end soon. Decide whether to buy it now or wait.

**Student A:**
You are excited about the offer and do not want to miss out. Explain why buying now seems worthwhile.

**Student B:**
You think the offer may encourage an impulsive decision. Suggest checking reviews, comparing alternatives, or sleeping on it before buying.

### Conversation Situation 5: A Class Boat Trip

**Situation:**
You are organising a class boat trip with a limited budget. You cannot afford all the food, entertainment, and comfort options. Agree on three priorities.

**Student A:**
You want to spend most of the budget on activities and entertainment. Explain what will make the trip memorable.

**Student B:**
You want to prioritise practical needs such as food, shade, and transport. Explain your concerns and negotiate a balanced plan.

### Conversation Situation 6: Helping or Being Paid

**Situation:**
A neighbour has asked for help with a small task and offered a small payment. Discuss whether to accept the money or help as a favour.

**Student A:**
You think payment recognises a person’s time and effort. Explain why accepting it can be fair.

**Student B:**
You think treating help as a favour can strengthen relationships. Explain why money might change the situation and agree on an appropriate response.

## UNIT 8 — Small Things Matter

### Conversation Situation 1: Sharing a Study Space

**Situation:**
You share a room or study space, but you have different ideas about tidiness. Agree on practical rules that both of you can follow.

**Student A:**
You need a neat environment to concentrate. Explain which habits bother you and suggest clear rules.

**Student B:**
You prefer a more relaxed environment and do not want to spend too much time cleaning. Explain your position and offer a compromise.

### Conversation Situation 2: Starting a Side Project

**Situation:**
One of you wants to start a small side project while studying. Discuss the idea and decide on a manageable first step.

**Student A:**
Describe your project, explain why you are interested, and say what support you need.

**Student B:**
Show interest, ask questions, and suggest ways to develop the idea without taking on too much.

### Conversation Situation 3: Too Many Commitments

**Situation:**
A student has agreed to several activities and now feels overwhelmed. Work together to make a realistic plan for the coming week.

**Student A:**
You are the overwhelmed student. Explain your commitments and why you find it difficult to give anything up.

**Student B:**
You are a friend. Suggest priorities, encourage the student to set limits, and help them choose one change.

### Conversation Situation 4: A Podcast About Stress

**Situation:**
You are creating a short podcast for university students about reducing everyday stress. Agree on its main message and two practical tips.

**Student A:**
You think the podcast should focus on organisation, planning, and small changes to routines. Explain your ideas.

**Student B:**
You think it should focus on rest, enjoyable activities, and asking for support. Explain your ideas and agree on a balanced episode.

### Conversation Situation 5: Making a Complaint

**Situation:**
A customer has an everyday problem with a product or service and wants to complain. Discuss what happened and agree on a practical response.

**Student A:**
You are the customer. Explain the problem clearly, say how it affects you, and suggest a fair solution.

**Student B:**
You represent the company. Show interest, ask for details, respond politely, and offer a practical solution.

### Conversation Situation 6: A New Routine

**Situation:**
You want to change your shared morning routine because you are often late and stressed. Agree on a plan for next week.

**Student A:**
You think careful preparation the evening before will solve the problem. Explain your plan.

**Student B:**
You think the plan needs to be simpler and more flexible. Suggest an alternative and agree on what each person will do.

## UNIT 9 — Things Happen

### Conversation Situation 1: A Missed Opportunity

**Situation:**
A student has missed a deadline for an opportunity they wanted and is upset. Discuss what happened and decide what to do next.

**Student A:**
You are the student. Explain the mistake, describe your regrets, and say why the opportunity mattered.

**Student B:**
You are a friend. Reassure the student, suggest possible next steps, and help them make a realistic plan.

### Conversation Situation 2: A Lucky Success

**Situation:**
You are discussing someone who succeeded after an unexpected opportunity. Decide how much of the success was due to luck and how much to effort.

**Student A:**
You think luck was the main reason. Explain how things might have been different without the opportunity.

**Student B:**
You think preparation and effort made the opportunity useful. Explain your view and acknowledge the role of chance.

### Conversation Situation 3: An Advice Letter

**Situation:**
A younger student has written that they chose an activity they now regret and feel unable to change their mind. Agree on advice for your reply.

**Student A:**
You think they should continue for a while before making another decision. Explain the possible benefits.

**Student B:**
You think they should consider changing direction. Explain the risks of continuing and agree on a supportive reply.

### Conversation Situation 4: Putting a Mistake Right

**Situation:**
One of you accidentally caused a problem in a group project. Discuss how to repair the situation before the deadline.

**Student A:**
Explain what went wrong, take responsibility, and suggest how you can help.

**Student B:**
Explain how the mistake affects the group, respond constructively, and help agree on a solution.

### Conversation Situation 5: A Risky Decision

**Situation:**
You have been offered a new opportunity, but accepting it means giving up a familiar and secure option. Agree on what to do.

**Student A:**
You want to take the risk. Explain the possible benefits and what you might regret if you refused.

**Student B:**
You prefer the secure option. Explain the possible difficulties and suggest a way to reduce the risk.

### Conversation Situation 6: Plans That Went Wrong

**Situation:**
Your plans for a trip have fallen through because of an unexpected problem. Decide on an alternative without blaming each other.

**Student A:**
You are disappointed and wish you had planned differently. Explain your concerns and suggest one alternative.

**Student B:**
You want to focus on what is still possible. Reassure your partner, suggest another alternative, and help reach agreement.
