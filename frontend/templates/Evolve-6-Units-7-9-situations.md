# Situations EVOLVE 6 (Units 7–9)

## UNIT 7 — Roots

### Conversation Situation 1: DNA Testing: Benefits and Risks

**Situation:**
Your university is considering offering an ancestry-testing project to students. Discuss the benefits and risks and decide what safeguards would be needed.

**Student A:**
You think the project could help students understand their ancestry. Explain the benefits and respond to concerns.

**Student B:**
You are concerned about privacy, interpretation, and the use of genetic data. Ask questions and suggest safeguards before reaching agreement.

### Conversation Situation 2: Keeping a Celebration Alive

**Situation:**
You are planning a cultural celebration for younger members of a community. Agree on a format that respects its heritage and attracts new participants.

**Student A:**
You want to preserve familiar rituals and customs. Explain their significance.

**Student B:**
You want to adapt the celebration to modern interests and include newcomers. Explain your ideas and negotiate a compromise.

### Conversation Situation 3: Family Traditions and Personal Choice

**Situation:**
A family disagrees about whether everyone should attend an annual celebration. Discuss a compromise that maintains relationships and respects different circumstances.

**Student A:**
You believe shared celebrations help keep family bonds strong. Explain why attendance matters.

**Student B:**
You believe participation should be a personal choice. Explain your concerns and suggest other ways to stay connected.

### Conversation Situation 4: Saving an Endangered Language

**Situation:**
A town can fund only one small cultural project this year. Decide how to support a local language that fewer young people use.

**Student A:**
You want to invest in classes and activities for young speakers. Explain the potential benefits.

**Student B:**
You want to prioritise recording stories and creating an accessible digital archive. Explain why this may be more practical and agree on a plan.

### Conversation Situation 5: A Place With Significance

**Situation:**
You are planning a visit for people interested in your community’s history. Choose a place and decide how to explain its significance.

**Student A:**
You want to visit a well-known historical landmark. Explain what visitors could learn.

**Student B:**
You prefer a less famous place connected with everyday community life. Explain its value and help agree on a visit.

### Conversation Situation 6: Family Bonds and Boundaries

**Situation:**
You are discussing whether relatives should make a greater effort than friends to repair a damaged relationship. Compare your views and agree on sensible limits.

**Student A:**
You think family ties create special responsibilities. Explain why making an effort can matter.

**Student B:**
You think relationships should depend on respectful behaviour rather than biology. Explain why boundaries can be necessary and look for common ground.

## UNIT 8 — Short

### Conversation Situation 1: Distractions While Studying

**Situation:**
Two students are preparing for an important deadline but keep getting distracted. Agree on a practical plan for the next study session.

**Student A:**
You want to block notifications and create a strict, uninterrupted schedule. Explain why this would help.

**Student B:**
You prefer shorter work periods with planned breaks and more flexibility. Explain your reasons and agree on a plan.

### Conversation Situation 2: Instinct or Analysis

**Situation:**
You need to choose between two unfamiliar options quickly. Discuss whether to trust your initial reaction or spend more time comparing them.

**Student A:**
You think your first impression is a useful guide. Explain why and recognise any risks.

**Student B:**
You prefer to examine evidence and alternatives. Ask questions and propose a quick decision-making process.

### Conversation Situation 3: An App That Improves Focus

**Situation:**
An app developer is presenting an app that limits digital distractions to a potential user. Discuss the features and decide whether the user would try it.

**Student A:**
You are the developer. Explain the app’s main selling points and how it helps users concentrate.

**Student B:**
You are a potential user. Ask about flexibility, convenience, and possible drawbacks, then explain your decision.

### Conversation Situation 4: A Pitch to an Investor

**Situation:**
A student entrepreneur is asking an investor to support a new service for university students. Discuss its value and agree on the next step.

**Student A:**
You are the entrepreneur. Give a concise pitch, describe the problem, and explain why your solution stands out.

**Student B:**
You are the investor. Ask about demand, costs, and competition. Explain what you would need to know before offering support.

### Conversation Situation 5: Improving Presentation Slides

**Situation:**
You are revising a short presentation about a new idea. You disagree about how much information should appear on the slides. Agree on a clear structure.

**Student A:**
You want the slides to contain detailed information so that the audience can follow the argument. Explain your reasons.

**Student B:**
You want a smaller number of clear messages and stronger visuals. Explain how this could make the pitch more persuasive.

### Conversation Situation 6: Choosing a Student App

**Situation:**
Your student group can recommend only one type of app to new students. Choose between a focus app, a planning app, and a social connection app.

**Student A:**
You think an app that improves concentration or organisation would be most useful. Explain the benefits.

**Student B:**
You think social connection is the more urgent need. Explain your view and negotiate which app to recommend first.

## UNIT 9 — Healthy vs. Modern Life

### Conversation Situation 1: A Sedentary University Day

**Situation:**
Your university wants to help students spend less time sitting. Discuss two possible measures and agree on a practical first step.

**Student A:**
You want scheduled movement breaks and more active classroom routines. Explain why the university should take responsibility.

**Student B:**
You prefer voluntary activities and better facilities rather than compulsory breaks. Explain your concerns and agree on a workable plan.

### Conversation Situation 2: Not Enough Sleep

**Situation:**
A student feels exhausted because study, work, and late-night screen use leave little time for sleep. Work together to identify realistic changes.

**Student A:**
You are the student. Explain your routine and why it seems difficult to change.

**Student B:**
You are a friend. Ask questions, suggest manageable changes, and help the student choose one priority.

### Conversation Situation 3: Probing Questions About Air Quality

**Situation:**
A journalist is interviewing a local official about air pollution. Discuss what is being done and seek a clear explanation of the next steps.

**Student A:**
You are the journalist. Ask specific questions about the problem, the evidence, and the proposed measures. Politely challenge vague answers.

**Student B:**
You are the official. Explain the measures and the constraints, ask for time to check details when necessary, and respond clearly without inventing facts.

### Conversation Situation 4: Traffic and Local Health

**Situation:**
Residents and a local representative are discussing a proposal to limit traffic near a busy street. Agree on a measure that considers health and access.

**Student A:**
You represent residents concerned about air quality and noise. Explain why action is needed.

**Student B:**
You represent people who depend on road access for work or business. Explain their concerns and negotiate a compromise.

### Conversation Situation 5: Choosing a Clean-Water Project

**Situation:**
A community has received funding for a small clean-water project. Discuss whether to prioritise new facilities or support for maintaining existing ones.

**Student A:**
You want to invest in new water points so that more households can gain access. Explain the benefits.

**Student B:**
You want to prioritise maintenance and training so that existing facilities remain reliable. Explain the long-term value and agree on a balanced plan.

### Conversation Situation 6: Healthy Choices for Different Needs

**Situation:**
You are planning a workplace or university wellbeing programme for people with different schedules and needs. Agree on two measures to introduce first.

**Student A:**
You want to focus on physical activity and reducing time spent sitting. Explain who would benefit.

**Student B:**
You want to focus on sleep, rest, and more flexible routines. Explain your priorities and reach a practical agreement.
