# Examination questions EVOLVE 5 (Units 7–9)

## UNIT 7 — Priorities

1. What makes an experience worthwhile? Do you value experiences more than possessions? Explain your priorities and give an example.

2. What makes a job fulfilling? Is meaningful work more important than a high salary? What compromises would you be willing to make?

3. How can people discover their calling? Is it better to follow a strong inclination or try different kinds of work first? Explain.

4. Why can having too many choices make a decision harder? Have you ever felt spoilt for choice? How did you reach a decision?

5. What kind of work would allow you to make a contribution to society? What would you find satisfying about it? What difficulties might you face?

6. Have you ever bought something that was not worth the money? Why did you buy it? What would you do differently now? You may use an invented example.

7. How do shops and online platforms encourage impulsive purchases? Which techniques are most effective? How can consumers resist them?

8. Can money buy happiness? Is it better to spend it on yourself, on other people, or on experiences? Support your view with an example.

9. When is cooperation more effective than competition in a negotiation? What makes a solution fair to both sides? Give an example.

10. Would you enjoy bargaining over a price? What would make you accept or reject an offer? How would you explain your decision politely?

11. When can financial rewards reduce a person’s willingness to help? How do social norms differ from market norms? Give a simple example.

12. How would you plan an enjoyable class trip on a limited budget? Which activities and extras would be worthwhile? What would you leave out and why?

## UNIT 8 — Small Things Matter

1. Would you describe yourself as neat or messy? How does your usual level of organisation affect your daily life? What would you like to improve?

2. Do you think a tidy room helps people concentrate? Can a messy environment sometimes encourage creativity? Explain your view.

3. Which small daily habits make the biggest difference to your mood or productivity? How did you develop them? Would you recommend them to others?

4. How do you organise your time when you have several tasks to complete? What do you tend to put off? How could you make your routine more manageable?

5. What side project would you like to start? Why does it interest you? What would you need to do before getting started?

6. How can a side project benefit someone who is studying or working full-time? What problems might it create? How can they maintain a balance?

7. What would you say to a friend who has an interesting idea but is afraid to try it? How could you show interest and encourage them without putting pressure on them?

8. What makes advice or a suggestion useful? Is it better to offer a clear solution or ask questions first? Explain with an example.

9. What everyday problem would make you write a complaint letter? How would you explain what went wrong? What response or solution would you request?

10. What usually helps you reduce everyday stress? Do you prefer practical changes, physical activity, or talking to someone? Explain why.

11. If you made a short podcast about reducing stress, what advice would you include? Who would it be for? How would you make it engaging?

12. What are you planning to change in your routine in the near future? What arrangements have you already made? What difficulties do you expect?

## UNIT 9 — Things Happen

1. What role does luck play in people’s lives? Is success mainly the result of chance or effort? Give an example to support your view.

2. How might your life be different if you had made a different choice in the past? What would probably have changed? You may use an imagined example.

3. Can a mistake lead to a positive result? Describe a real or invented situation. What could someone learn from it?

4. How do you usually react when you realise you have made a mistake? Is it easy for you to admit it? What helps you put things right?

5. What advice would you give someone who keeps thinking about a past mistake? How could they learn from it without blaming themselves too much?

6. What is an effective way to reassure a friend who is worried about a problem? What should you avoid saying? Give an example.

7. What do you wish were different about your current routine or circumstances? What could you realistically change? Explain your priorities.

8. Is it better to take a risk and possibly regret it or to avoid the risk altogether? Does your answer depend on the situation? Explain.

9. How can an unexpected event change someone’s plans? Describe a possible example and explain how the person could respond.

10. If you could give your younger self one piece of advice, what would it be? Why would it have been useful? You may answer generally rather than personally.

11. What makes an advice letter helpful and reassuring? How would you respond to someone who feels that one setback has ruined their plans?

12. When someone succeeds by chance, does that make the achievement less valuable? How can people make the most of an unexpected opportunity?
