# Evolve 5 · Unit 7

Adapted from the teacher’s Unit 7 slides, including class debates.

## Essay · about 250 words

Write an essay of about 250 words: Meaningful work matters more than a high salary. To what extent do you agree? Consider both perspectives, support your argument with an example, and give a reasoned conclusion. Use your own words. Do not copy the reading preview or task sentences.

## Individual questions · no preparation

1. What makes an experience worthwhile? Do you value experiences more than possessions? Explain your priorities and give an example.

2. What makes a job fulfilling? Is meaningful work more important than a high salary? What compromises would you be willing to make?

3. How can people discover their calling? Is it better to follow a strong inclination or try different kinds of work first? Explain.

4. Why can having too many choices make a decision harder? Have you ever felt spoilt for choice? How did you reach a decision?

5. What kind of work would allow you to make a contribution to society? What would you find satisfying about it? What difficulties might you face?

6. Have you ever bought something that was not worth the money? Why did you buy it? What would you do differently now? You may use an invented example.

7. How do shops and online platforms encourage impulsive purchases? Which techniques are most effective? How can consumers resist them?

8. Can money buy happiness? Is it better to spend it on yourself, on other people, or on experiences? Support your view with an example.

9. When is cooperation more effective than competition in a negotiation? What makes a solution fair to both sides? Give an example.

10. Would you enjoy bargaining over a price? What would make you accept or reject an offer? How would you explain your decision politely?

11. When can financial rewards reduce a person’s willingness to help? How do social norms differ from market norms? Give a simple example.

12. How would you plan an enjoyable class trip on a limited budget? Which activities and extras would be worthwhile? What would you leave out and why?

## Dialogue cards · one minute preparation

Conversation Situation: Meaningful Work or a High Salary

Situation:
You are advising a graduate who has two job offers: one pays well but is not very interesting; the other is meaningful but pays less. Agree on which offer to recommend.

Student A:
You think financial security should come first. Explain the benefits of the higher salary and respond to your partner’s concerns.

Student B:
You think fulfilment and personal values should come first. Explain the benefits of meaningful work and suggest a realistic compromise.

---

Conversation Situation: A Purchase Worth Making

Situation:
You have a limited budget and are deciding how to spend it. You can buy a useful object or pay for a memorable experience. Agree on one choice.

Student A:
You prefer a practical object that can be used for a long time. Explain its value and question the benefits of the experience.

Student B:
You prefer an experience that can create lasting memories. Explain its value and respond to concerns about practicality.

---

Conversation Situation: Negotiating a Better Deal

Situation:
A customer wants to buy a second-hand bicycle, but the asking price is above their budget. Negotiate a deal that both people can accept.

Student A:
You are the customer. Ask for a lower price, give reasons, and offer a compromise.

Student B:
You are the seller. Explain why the bicycle is worth the price, respond to the offer, and decide what you can include in the deal.

---

Conversation Situation: An Impulsive Purchase

Situation:
One of you wants to buy an expensive item immediately because a website says the offer will end soon. Decide whether to buy it now or wait.

Student A:
You are excited about the offer and do not want to miss out. Explain why buying now seems worthwhile.

Student B:
You think the offer may encourage an impulsive decision. Suggest checking reviews, comparing alternatives, or sleeping on it before buying.

---

Conversation Situation: A Class Boat Trip

Situation:
You are organising a class boat trip with a limited budget. You cannot afford all the food, entertainment, and comfort options. Agree on three priorities.

Student A:
You want to spend most of the budget on activities and entertainment. Explain what will make the trip memorable.

Student B:
You want to prioritise practical needs such as food, shade, and transport. Explain your concerns and negotiate a balanced plan.

---

Conversation Situation: Helping or Being Paid

Situation:
A neighbour has asked for help with a small task and offered a small payment. Discuss whether to accept the money or help as a favour.

Student A:
You think payment recognises a person’s time and effort. Explain why accepting it can be fair.

Student B:
You think treating help as a favour can strengthen relationships. Explain why money might change the situation and agree on an appropriate response.
