Teacher Evolve 6 Unit 7 ROOTS slides: SB pp.65–74; DNA privacy debate; family traditions discussion; dying languages; family bonds debate and one-minute exit task. Adapted assessment prompts.

Essay (about 250 words)
Write an essay of about 250 words: Our roots influence us, but they do not define us. To what extent do you agree? Discuss the roles of upbringing, inherited background and personal choice. Consider a contrasting perspective and reach a reasoned conclusion. You may use general or invented examples; personal family disclosure is not required. Do not copy task sentences or the reading preview.

Individual answer: no preparation, 60–90 seconds
1. Would you want to take a DNA ancestry test? Explain the possible benefits and concerns.
2. Which shapes identity more strongly: upbringing or genes? Support your view.
3. When can a family tradition provide connection, and when can it create pressure?
4. What makes a place significant to a person or a community? You may use an invented example.
5. Should endangered languages be actively preserved? Explain your reasoning.
6. Do family ties create stronger obligations than friendships? Explain and qualify your view.

Dialogue: one minute preparation, 2–3 minutes discussion
1. A community is planning a cultural celebration for younger residents. Student A wants to preserve familiar rituals; Student B wants a more inclusive modern format. Agree on two activities and explain how they respect the heritage.
2. A university is considering offering DNA ancestry tests to students. Student A argues for the educational benefits; Student B raises privacy and interpretation concerns. Agree on safeguards and decide whether the project should go ahead.
3. A town has a small cultural budget. Student A wants to support an endangered local language; Student B wants to support a widely attended annual celebration. Negotiate an allocation and justify it.
4. A family disagrees about attendance at its annual celebration. Student A values shared obligations; Student B values individual boundaries. Agree on a compromise that preserves relationships without requiring anyone to disclose private experiences.
